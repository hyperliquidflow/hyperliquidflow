# What the prize is worth: capacity of the positioning factor

Both external reviews said to price the success case before committing a
quarter to confirmation runs, because the answer might reroute the effort. This
is that calculation. It prices the prize; it does not claim it, because the
edge is still unconfirmed (traded book t 1.42 frozen).

## Method

`scripts/capacity-study.ts` reads the legs the factor actually holds today from
`factor_shadow`, pulls the live order book for each, and walks it level by
level to get the real round-trip execution cost at each size
(`lib/slippage.ts`, 10 tests). Fee 4.5 bps per side, verified.

One defect was caught and fixed before these numbers were trusted: the first
version averaged in names whose book ran out, which prices only the portion
that fit and understates the cost of size exactly where size is the question.
It made the edge look like it survived to a $5M book. An exhausted name is now
excluded and counted as unfillable, which is what changed the answer.

## Result, 2026-08-12, 10 legs

| per name | book | legs fillable | exec bps | net bps/day | $/day | $/year |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $1k | $10k | 10/10 | 11.8 | 46.2 | $46 | $16,877 |
| $5k | $50k | 10/10 | 16.3 | 41.7 | $209 | $76,139 |
| **$10k** | **$100k** | **10/10** | **21.6** | **36.4** | **$364** | **$132,794** |
| $25k | $200k | 8/10 | 21.6 | 36.4 | $727 | partial book |
| $50k | $350k | 7/10 | 20.4 | 37.6 | $1,316 | partial book |
| $100k | $500k | 5/10 | 18.4 | 39.6 | $1,982 | partial book |

Rows below the bold line are not a larger version of this strategy. The thin
names exhaust first (STBL and RENDER at $25k, MELANIA at $50k, HYPE and KAS at
$100k), so scaling past the ceiling silently drops the legs that carry the
signal and leaves a different, less diversified book.

## Reading

**Honest capacity is about $100k of book, ten names at $10k each, worth roughly
$364 a day if the edge is real.** Annualised that is $133k, which is a return
above 100% and should be read as a warning rather than a promise: it is
arithmetic on an unconfirmed 39 bps/day edge, and an edge a third that size
pays a third as much. Execution cost rises from 11.8 bps to 21.6 bps between a
$10k and a $100k book, so roughly a fifth of the gross edge is eaten by depth
before the ceiling is reached.

Two consequences worth carrying into the decision:

1. **Capacity is set by the factor's taste for thin coins.** It leans into
   micro-cap perpetuals, which is plausibly where the inefficiency lives and
   certainly where the depth is not. A variant restricted to liquid names would
   scale further and might carry less edge. That trade is measurable on the
   existing cache and is now a named follow-up.
2. **The scale answers the strategic question the reviews raised.** At a $100k
   ceiling this is a personal-capital strategy, not a fund, and not a business
   on its own. If the goal is a business rather than a personal return, the
   instrument matters more than the trade, which is the argument for the
   intelligence product path rather than the trading path. If the goal is a
   personal return, $100k of book is a real and reachable target.

## Caveats

Live books at one instant, one snapshot of one day's legs. Depth varies with
time of day and regime, and a real book pays this cost every day it turns over.
No market-impact decay is modelled beyond the visible book, and resting orders
rather than crossing would lower the cost at the price of fill uncertainty.
