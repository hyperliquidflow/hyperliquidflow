What the study actually shows

What this language buys

  • +Density without noise. Hairline rules and a single ground let five columns sit together without the eye having to fight card borders, glass blur and a shadow on every block.
  • +Hierarchy comes from size, not chrome. A 40px page title against 14px body does the work that a bordered header currently does, and costs nothing to render.
  • +The warm neutral reads calmer. Notion's ink is a brown black, not grey. On a data page that difference is most of why it feels quiet rather than clinical.
  • +Restraint suits the new posture. A product that just retracted its edge claims is better served by a document than by a trading terminal.

What it costs

  • -Live state gets quiet. There is no real place for a pulsing dot or a teal active rail here. Freshness has to be carried by text, which is weaker for something polling every 60 seconds.
  • -The 900px column fights wide tables. Market Radar and the leaderboard want full bleed. Notion answers this with horizontal scroll, which is worse than the current full width layout.
  • -It is not a distinctive identity. This looks like every Notion adjacent tool, which is the opposite of what the current dark aura and grain were built for.
  • -Nothing here is token compatible. Different scale, different neutrals, different fonts. Adopting any of it means a token migration, not a page edit.

The part worth stealing

Two ideas survive contact with the existing system without a rewrite. Page properties, the key and value rows under the title, are a better home for scan time, regime and cohort size than another row of stat cards. And the callout block is a clean pattern for the standing caveat this product now has to carry on every page.
