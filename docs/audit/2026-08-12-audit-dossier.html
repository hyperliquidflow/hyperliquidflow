1. Executive summary

HyperliquidFLOW watches roughly 60 carefully filtered trading accounts on the Hyperliquid derivatives exchange, currently controlling $31.5M of their own equity, and tries to turn what they do into trading signals. The project's four-month history divides into two eras. Until 2026-08-08, the system looked alive and was not: every pipeline that could have measured whether its signals made money was silently dead, and a full audit concluded that no measured edge existed and none was measurable. Since 2026-08-08, the project has rebuilt itself into an honest measurement instrument and has used it aggressively. This dossier reports what that instrument now says.

The five verdicts that matter
  • Supported  Wallet skill ranking carries real signal, thinly. On a retrospective instrument, wallets with enough history to score (2,740 of 6,624) rank-correlate with future risk-adjusted performance at 0.0939, clean of lookahead; the point estimate clears the project's 0.08 bar, the confidence interval does not yet. The pre-registered live version of this test stands at 0 of 30 measurements, verdict around October.
  • Disproven  Short-hold entry copying, and coordination, do not make money. Tested across 120 days and 2,403 independent coin-days: negative after costs at every hold inside four hours even with zero latency, and coordination between wallets shows no dose-response at any hold. Full-pool long holds were statistically indistinguishable from zero, which is inconclusive rather than disproven.
  • Open  Two leads are alive, pending pre-registered tests. Copying what the cohort holds (not when it trades) predicts next-day relative returns at t near 3. And on an activity-matched pool, long holds of 24 to 72 hours stay positive after an overlap correction (t 2.6 to 3.1). Both were mined from the same 120-day cache; neither is proven, and the out-of-sample tests that decide them are already written down.
  • Not built  The system cannot place a real trade, and holds no real capital. There is no order code, no signing key, no exchange write path anywhere in the codebase. This is deliberate and gated by written criteria.
  • Sound  Security and operations pass review. Zero exploitable findings. Infrastructure runs on free tiers at roughly $0/month, with monitoring that now fails loudly instead of silently.

The honest bottom line for the goal of making money: today the system makes no money, cannot trade, and has not yet proven an edge worth trading. What it has that most projects like it never get: a measurement layer that has already killed two false hopes cheaply, two specific live leads with named scripts and named thresholds that will confirm or kill them, and written criteria for when to stop. Section 13 lays out the paths from here in money terms.

ELI5

Imagine you built a telescope to watch 60 of the best traders you could find, hoping to copy their trades and profit. For months the telescope was secretly broken and its dashboard said everything was fine. It got fixed in early August. Now that you can finally see clearly, the answer so far is: copying their trades loses a little money every time, because by the time you see a trade, the price has already moved. But two other ideas look promising: copying what they own rather than what they do, and holding copied positions for days rather than hours. Neither is proven yet. Also important: the machine has no money in it and no ability to trade. It is a research instrument, and now a trustworthy one.

2. What the system is

HyperliquidFLOW is a web application plus a set of scheduled jobs. It has no users other than its owner. Its parts:

  • Discovery and filtering. A nightly job (GitHub Actions, 00:00 UTC) scans the Hyperliquid leaderboard and fill history, has recorded 39,972 wallets over the project's life, and pushes each candidate through nine activation gates (win rate, trade count, profit factor, drawdown, equity, leverage, regime coverage, and others), plus wash-trading and Sybil-cluster detection, plus behavior profiling. Survivors become the "cohort": currently 59 wallets.
  • Scoring. Each cohort wallet gets a 0-to-1 score built from four factors: a Sharpe-style risk-adjusted return proxy, PnL consistency, drawdown behavior, and fit to the current market regime (lib/cohort-engine.ts).
  • Live watching. The cohort's open positions are snapshotted every few minutes around the clock (an uptime service pings the app every 5 minutes; a stale cache triggers a refresh). Snapshots land in Postgres (cohort_snapshots).
  • Signals. Six rule-based "recipes" read consecutive snapshots and emit events like "several high-scored wallets just added to the same coin" (lib/signal-lab.ts). A conviction gate restricts signals to 12 coins that carry roughly 90% of cohort capital.
  • Grading. Every signal is later scored as if it had been traded: entry at signal price, exit at a stop, a target, or a time limit, charged 7 bps round-trip cost plus slippage and funding, benchmarked against BTC scaled by each coin's beta. Results accumulate in signal_outcomes.
  • Research layer. Separate read-only scripts answer strategy questions offline from a local cache of 120 days of trading history, without waiting months for live signals to accumulate. This layer produced most of what Section 7 reports.
  • Interfaces. A dashboard (overview, leaderboard, signal feed, market radar, per-wallet lookup), a read-only Telegram bot for status checks, and a watchdog that messages the owner only when a health check changes state.

Architecture in one line: Next.js 15 app on Vercel, Postgres on Supabase, a KV cache for hot reads, GitHub Actions for heavy nightly compute, and the Hyperliquid public info API as the sole data source. All computation is server-side; the browser is a thin poller.

ELI5

Think of it as a fishing operation. A big net (discovery) pulls in thousands of traders. A series of sieves (gates) throws back all but about 60 proven ones. A camera watches what those 60 do all day. A rulebook (recipes) turns their behavior into "maybe buy this" notes. A scorekeeper (grading) checks later whether each note would have made money after fees. And a lab (research scripts) replays months of old footage to test new ideas overnight instead of waiting months to find out.

3. Can it make money today?

Three questions decide this, and all three have clean answers.

3.1 Can it place a trade? No.

Verified by direct code inspection across the entire repository: there is no order-placement code path, no exchange write endpoint, no signing library, and no key material. package.json contains no wallet or signing dependency. The Hyperliquid client (lib/hyperliquid-api-client.ts) posts exclusively to the read-only info endpoint; the trading endpoint is never referenced. No private-key variable exists in any environment file, example file, or CI secret list. Making the system trade would require building a signer, key storage, and an execution path from scratch. This is by design: the roadmap gates automated execution behind a written decision that itself sits behind paper-trading results that do not exist yet.

3.2 Does it hold or manage real capital? No.

The $31.5M this dossier mentions belongs to the watched traders, not to the project. The only trading-like feature is paper trading, which lives entirely in the owner's browser storage, charges no fees, models no slippage, and has no server record. The 2026-08-08 audit explicitly lists "paper trading as validation" among unproven claims, and this dossier concurs: the current paper feature is a toy, not evidence.

3.3 Does it have a proven edge worth trading? Not yet.

The founding premise, "follow what skilled wallets do," was tested three ways over 120 days and 2,403 independent coin-days (the honest unit of evidence; see Section 9) and failed each test at short holds (Section 7.2). Two successor ideas are alive and unproven (Section 7.3). The live signal recipes have 29 graded outcomes against a 30-outcome minimum per recipe before any verdict is allowed (Section 8). So the strict answer is: there is nothing today that written project standards would allow trading.

What stands between today and the first dollar
  1. A confirmed edge: one of the two open leads surviving out-of-sample confirmation, or a live recipe reaching 30 graded outcomes with positive expectancy.
  2. A validation layer: server-side paper trading with real cost modeling (already specified as roadmap Phase 3, gated on step 1).
  3. An execution layer: signer, key management, order placement, risk limits. Does not exist; deliberately last.
  4. Capital and a written risk decision, which the project's own kill criteria require to be made from documented numbers, not conviction.
ELI5

Three questions: Does the machine have hands to trade with? No, on purpose. Does it hold anyone's money? No. Does it know something the market doesn't? Not provably, yet. It has two promising hunches and a strict house rule: no real money until a hunch survives every test, then fake-money trading with honest costs, and only then a deliberate decision to build hands.

4. What went wrong before

External reviewers should know this history because it explains both the project's paranoia and why its current numbers deserve more trust than its old ones.

4.1 The 2026-08-08 audit verdict

"No measured edge exists, and none was ever measurable. In four months the system produced zero rank IC measurements, zero surviving graded signal outcomes, and zero recipe calibrations. [...] The machinery is well built; integration and observability failed, silently, in five specific places."

The five root causes, each verified with file-level evidence at the time:

  1. A phantom database column (since 2026-04-19). The nightly scan selected a column that never existed. The error was swallowed and the job stayed green for 64 runs, so the score-history table that all skill measurement depends on had zero rows, ever.
  2. GitHub disabled all four scheduled workflows on 2026-06-22. GitHub turns off schedules after 60 days without a commit. The watchdog was itself one of the disabled workflows. No failure email exists for an auto-disable, so nothing alerted. The cohort then decayed from 493 wallets to 58 as hygiene kept pruning with no scan refilling.
  3. The real heartbeat was an unmonitored third-party pinger, which died around 2026-07-16. Refresh cadence fell to once per day, which structurally killed 3 recipes outright and starved 7 more; only the 3 funding-based ones could still fire, at roughly one signal per week.
  4. A learning deadlock. Signal outcomes were deleted after 30 days, while the learning job refused to start below 100 accumulated outcomes: a gate that could never open at the actual signal rate. A separate bug stamped 4-hour outcomes with 25-hour prices.
  5. Expected-value circularity. The script meant to calibrate signal quality from real outcomes was never wired into any workflow, so signal EV silently fell back to backtest win rates: the exact circular logic it was built to remove.

Compounding all five, the dashboard fabricated health: a permanently green monitoring dot, a hardcoded "ok" pipeline card, and stale data restamped with the current time. An earlier audit (2026-07-22) failed precisely here: it reported two empty tables as populated without querying them.

ELI5

The factory's machines had been unplugged for months, but the control room lights were painted green. Nobody lied on purpose; the system was built to look healthy rather than to prove it was healthy. The August audit unplugged the painted panel and wired every light to the actual machine. The most important cultural change: now, when something fails, it is designed to fail loudly.

5. The rebuild and the grading loop

Phases 0 and 1 of the rebuild roadmap shipped on 2026-08-08/09 and are verified in production: all five workflows active, a monthly keepalive commit preventing the 60-day auto-disable, a 5-minute external heartbeat that emails on failure, per-outcome grading replacing the deadlocked batch gate, recipes cut from 15 to 6, and an honesty pass that put a real data age on every screen. The test suite grew from 312 to 523 passing tests, including first-ever coverage of the signal engine and the scoring formula.

5.1 The grading loop had to be rebuilt twice

The 2026-08-09 claim "grading loop verified working" was retracted a day later. Rows were being written, but the simulator exited on the first available price, so every trade was graded as a 1-hour time exit; stops and targets could never trigger. The rebuilt loop (migration 022) walks the hourly candle path, uses point-in-time volatility, and charges slippage and funding. Regrading moved measured expectancy from +2.5 bps to -97.4 bps per signal at an unchanged 48.1% win rate. The comfortable number was the broken one.

5.2 Four deliberate regrades

Migrations 022 through 025 each wiped all existing grades and regraded from scratch, as the pricing logic improved: true horizons, then a BTC benchmark, then a beta-scaled benchmark, then a recorded stop/target structure. Each reset archived what it replaced (a superseded-rows table, reversible). The cost is a small live sample today; the benefit is that the 29 outcomes that now exist are priced under one consistent, defensible method.

5.3 The exit structure was retuned on evidence

A path simulation over 2,808 random entries showed the original exit (2 ATR stop, 3 ATR target) timed out 74% of trades and hit its target 6.9% of the time. The shipped structure is now 1 ATR both sides: 84% of trades resolve at their levels, and outcome dispersion fell from 285 to 195 bps, which roughly halves the sample needed to detect a given edge. Section 7.3 notes the tension this creates with the long-hold lead.

ELI5

The scorekeeper was rebuilt. The first version accidentally gave every trade the same easy grade, which made results look mildly positive. The honest version says the signals so far would have lost about 1% per trade. That sounds like bad news, and it is, but it is the kind of bad news that saves money: you learn it on paper instead of on a brokerage statement. The scoring rules were then rewritten four times, each time re-marking every old trade, so today's records are small but clean.

6.1 Why 59 is a recovery, not a decline

Yesterday's headline count was 76, but 49 of those 76 held exactly $0. Two defects caused this, both found and fixed on 2026-08-11/12:

  • Unfunded activation. The scoring gate read live equity only from the leaderboard snapshot, so wallets absent from that day's leaderboard were activated without any balance check. The fix (scan Phase 9b) queries the exchange directly for the activated set. Its first run cut 147 unfunded wallets. Confirmed live: deactivations in the last 48 hours show unfunded: 147.
  • A liquidation gate measuring the wrong thing. The old gate used free margin as a proxy for liquidation risk. A trader deploying their full balance scored zero and was ejected as "liquidation imminent" while sitting 25% from any liquidation price. Of 36 wallets removed on that gate in 48 hours, 30 were false positives holding $7.0M between them. The gate now measures actual distance to the liquidation price. Intraday liquidation-gate removals collapsed from 36 per 48 hours to single digits.

The signal-producing cohort (funded, holding) went from 26 to 56 in the stored-snapshot replay that validated the fix; today's live query reads 57. 100% of today's active wallets are funded, against 34% two days ago. What the recovery has done for signal volume is not yet measurable, and this dossier declines to credit it with the recent jump in outcome rows (9 on 08-10, 14 on 08-11, against roughly 0.3 per day before): the 08-10 rows predate the cohort fixes entirely, and the 08-11 rows are fourteen entries of one recipe on a single coin, plausibly one episode counted repeatedly, the exact duplicate pattern Section 8 warns about. The first clean read of post-recovery signal volume comes from replaying the days after 2026-08-12 through the recipes.

6.2 The remaining size problem

The system had 493 active wallets in April. Recipes that need several wallets to coordinate scale with roughly the square of cohort size, so today's 59 produce few coordination events. The project measured the obvious fix, tightening activation on trading activity, and deliberately rejected it: the thresholds that lift scoring quality would cut the live cohort to 27 wallets or fewer and cost roughly 98% of pair-coordination events. The stated direction instead: discovery should prefer wallets that trade on many distinct days, and the gate question gets revisited when the cohort is comfortably larger.

ELI5

The watch list said 76 traders, but 49 of those accounts were empty shells; only 26 could actually move money. Two broken filters caused it: one let empty accounts onto the list, the other kicked off good traders for a made-up reason. Both are fixed. The list now says 59, and all 59 are real, funded traders, together holding about $31 million. Smaller number, far healthier list. The next problem is growth: 59 is too few for signals that need several traders to agree at once.

7. The evidence: proven, disproven, open

This is the heart of the dossier. Every result here comes from named, re-runnable scripts, and each headline number is reported with its sample size and its caveats. Terms used below: bps is basis points, hundredths of a percent. IC (information coefficient) is a rank correlation between a prediction and an outcome; 0 is worthless, 0.05 is faint, 0.10 is genuinely useful in this domain. t is a signal-to-noise score; roughly, above 2 means unlikely to be luck, below 2 means unproven.

7.1 Supported  Wallet skill ranking carries real signal, thinly

Question: does the project's wallet score actually predict future performance? For four months this was unmeasurable (the history table was empty). The answer now comes from scripts/cohort-skill-test.ts: score each wallet on the first half of its own daily PnL series, then rank-correlate against the second half, expressed in each wallet's own risk units so account size cannot masquerade as skill.

One scope note: the live cohort skews less active than this instrument's pool, so the live gate may read lower.

Question: if you enter where a cohort wallet entered, benchmarked against the market and net of 7 bps round-trip cost, do you make money? The instrument is scripts/fill-study.ts over a cached 120 days of fills. The split-half test showed the 45-day positives were regime, not skill: the two halves of the 120-day window carried opposite signs, each statistically significant, which is what a market-direction artifact looks like. Precision about what this table kills: holds inside four hours are disproven (negative with strong t-values, even at zero latency); holds beyond four hours on this full pool are indistinguishable from zero, which is inconclusive, and Section 7.3 revisits exactly that region on a cleaner pool. What is real: the cohort's entries do drift in their favor from the fill price (+14 bps, t 12.6, flat across horizons). That is genuine but useless to a follower: it is market exposure, and stripping the benchmark and charging costs consumes it entirely.

Coordination (several wallets entering the same coin and direction within 2 hours) was the premise under all six live recipes. Tested at N = 1 through 5 wallets: no dose-response. If agreement carried information, the effect should strengthen with N; instead N = 3 and N = 4 are the worst rows at short holds, and the one attractive cell (N = 2 at 24h) reproduces identically at N = 1, where there is no coordination at all. The project recorded the correct conclusion: continuing to tune entry-copy recipes is not supported by the measurement.

7.3 Open  Two live leads, neither proven

Lead one: positioning, not timing. scripts/positioning-factor.ts tests a different expression of the same wallets' skill: each day, rank coins by how hard the cohort leans into them, and hold that book. This converts a timing bet (which failed) into a holdings bet, and amortizes trading costs over days. Result: cohort exposure level predicts next-day relative returns at IC 0.0640 (t 3.12) across 104 days, positive on 61% of days. The project calls it, correctly, "the strongest signal this project has measured." A traded long-short version reaches only t 1.92 at its best width (about 37 to 47 bps/day gross mean), and the split-half is same-signed but front-loaded (61.4 bps then 14.1). Unproven; measured honestly; the next test is out-of-sample confirmation.

Lead two: long holds on an activity-matched pool. The newest fill-study run bands the wallet pool to the scan's own activity range (60 to 1000 trades), which removes market makers and dormant accounts. On that pool, following entries turns positive at every hold past 12 hours with both split halves agreeing, the first time that has happened. The commit reporting this also flagged its own weakness: 72-hour windows overlap, so consecutive entries on the same coin share a price path and the t-statistics overcount. The correction, entries spaced at least one full hold apart per coin so no two observations share a price path, was committed as a check that had to pass before the lead could be taken seriously.

The correction was run twice on 2026-08-12, by two different operators: once by the research session that built it, once by this audit. The outputs match row for row. Stated precisely, that match is a determinism check, not independent confirmation: both runs used the same code on the same cache, so it establishes that the pipeline has no hidden state, and nothing about whether the cache is faithful to the exchange or the statistic is the right one. Why this is a lead and not a proof, in five parts. One window, one regime: 120 days in which BTC fell 15.3% (as measured by this run on its own pool). Skewed payoff: win rates near 50% with strongly positive means say the return is carried by a right tail of large winners; realizing it requires letting winners run, which matters for sizing later. An untested cost term: the 7 bps charged here is two taker fees and nothing else. The research tables charge no slippage and, more importantly for multi-day holds, no funding: crowded alt perpetuals pay funding daily, often on exactly the side the cohort is leaning, so the 48 and 72 hour means could shrink materially once funding is charged. The live grading system charges funding and slippage; the research layer does not yet. Survivorship in the pool: the study draws wallets from today's discovery table, and discovery is performance-conditioned, so replayed windows include wallets a real-time follower could not have known about at the time. Freezing pool membership as of window start is a required control in the next run. Selection pressure: this table sits inside a research program that examined dozens of cells, grids, and sweeps on the same cache; two survivors at t near 3 must be read against that family. The post-correction split-half has also not been run yet. Every one of these is addressed by the next experiment already on the books: a 200-day fetch, four sub-period splits instead of two, and pre-registered hypotheses. Note also the direct design tension: the live grading system's 1 ATR exits optimize for fast resolution, while this measurement says the return, if real, lives at 24 to 72 hours.

Two candidates this audit can close. First, the point-in-time score decile table (scores rebuilt from each wallet's history strictly before each trade, no lookahead) produced its first recorded output in this audit's re-run: no monotone pattern across deciles at a 4-hour hold, all t-values below 1.6. The "fade the weakest wallets" idea has no support at short holds. Second, the combined-features experiment (scripts/signal-stack.ts) settled its own question in the closing hours of 2026-08-12: an equal-weight blend of all seven features scored IC 0.0006, because six of the seven are cohort-derived and highly correlated (entry flow and its inverse at -0.88); a feature-subset sweep then showed nothing improves on the cohort-lean feature alone (IC 0.0627, t 3.2), which is the same positioning idea as lead one computed a second way from the same cache: corroboration of the code path, not an independent confirmation. Funding at its hypothesized mean-reversion sign actively hurts the stack (crowded positioning continued rather than reversed); the sign-flipped variant reads better but was chosen after seeing the data, so the project has quarantined it as a hypothesis for the next, longer run to confirm or kill out of sample. That refusal is the discipline of Section 9 working as intended.

ELI5

Three findings. One: the system's "who is skilled" rankings are real; skilled traders stay skilled a little more often than chance. Two: copying a skilled trader's purchase doesn't work at short holds; by the time you see it, the easy money is gone, and even perfectly instant copying loses after fees. Three: two ideas still look good. Copy the portfolio (what they hold overall) instead of the purchases. And if you do copy a purchase, hold it for two or three days, not two or three hours; the data says patience is where any remaining profit lives. Both ideas passed their first hard test. Passed a test, not all the tests: the remaining ones include real funding costs, a fair replay that only uses traders you could have known about at the time, and fresh data the ideas have never seen.

The house rule says no verdict on any rulebook before 30 scored notes each, like refusing to judge a baseball player on ten at-bats. The pipeline that produces notes just got 30 times faster, so the verdicts will arrive within weeks now, not years. Expect several rulebooks to be deleted; the rules say deleted, not tweaked.

10. Security review

A focused security review was performed as part of this audit (code-level, whole attack surface, high-confidence findings only). Result: zero findings met the exploitability bar. Posture, as verified in code:

  • Every endpoint that can trigger work or write data is authenticated with a constant-time secret comparison: the two cron endpoints and the watchdog check a bearer secret; production refuses to boot if that secret is unset, so the check can never run against an empty value.
  • The Telegram webhook fails closed and is double-gated: a secret header check (rejects everything if the secret is unconfigured), then a single-chat allowlist, with strangers receiving deliberate silence. Its five commands are read-only, dispatched from a fixed list; message text never reaches a query, a shell, or a URL.
  • The database is default-deny. Row-level security is enabled on all application tables with zero policies, so the public API key sees no rows; the privileged service key exists only server-side, with no client component touching the database. The one browser-exposed variable is a cosmetic poll interval.
  • No injection surface was found: all queries go through a parameterized builder, there is no shell execution or dynamic code evaluation anywhere in the application, and no unsafe HTML rendering in any component. User-supplied wallet addresses are format-validated before use.
  • The 15 public read-only data routes are a deliberate design choice, not an oversight. They serve analytics computed from public on-chain data; that is the product. Documented as such in the repository.
  • CI carries no untrusted-input triggers (schedule and manual dispatch only). Blast-radius note: repository write access equals data-plane access, since CI holds the service keys; inherent to the design and worth remembering when granting collaborators.

Items examined and rejected as non-findings, listed for completeness: a dev-mode auth bypass unreachable in deployed environments; error messages that relay upstream text without secret material; the open data routes acting as a proxy for public data; a header length pre-check that leaks only secret length; and a cache-control inconsistency on one route that is a correctness quirk, not a vulnerability.

ELI5

Could a stranger break in, change data, or steal secrets? The review says no: every door that can change anything needs a key, the keys are checked in a tamper-resistant way, the database refuses to talk to strangers entirely, and the windows everyone can look through show only information that is already public on the blockchain. The one caution: anyone allowed to edit the project's code inherits its keys, so collaborator access is the real perimeter.

12. Governance: gates and kill criteria

Unusually for a solo project, the rules for continuing or stopping are written down, dated, and pre-committed, with the explicit clause that changing one's mind after seeing results is not grounds for amendment.

12.1 The standing thresholds

  • Wallet scoring: rolling rank IC must exceed 0.08 (the computed minimum detectable IC for this cohort size) once 30 daily live measurements exist. Below that, with p above 0.05, the premise is revisited: no scoring rewrites, no paper trader.
  • Recipes: at 30 graded outcomes each, positive net expectancy survives; anything else is deleted, not tuned. The suspended funding recipe needs non-negative expectancy at 30 to return to the feed.
  • Scoring V2 canary: a shadow scoring formula runs beside V1; at 30 shadow measurements the better one wins, decided around 2026-09-10. (Counting starts 2026-08-11: the canary silently wrote nothing for months due to a rejected upsert, another swallowed error found and fixed on 2026-08-10.)
  • Paper trading (future Phase 3): kill at a 30% drawdown at any point, or 90-day underperformance against a random risk-matched cohort or simply holding BTC. The benchmarks are named in advance.
  • Latency: if measured signal-to-write latency exceeds what a recipe's premise tolerates, that recipe's verdict is "not at this latency," which counts as a kill.

13. The path to money

The owner's stated goal is to make money or build a system that helps do so. Four honest paths exist. They are not mutually exclusive, but they compete for the same scarce resource: owner attention.

Path A: Prove one of the two leads, then trade it small

The shortest credible path. The positioning factor (Section 7.3) is the strongest candidate: its diagnostic reads t near 3.1, measured two ways from the same cache (one finding computed twice, not two confirmations), its traded version reaches t 1.92 gross and improves with book width, and confirming or killing it needs a longer window and an out-of-sample split, which are days of work, not months. The long-hold lead is second: it passed the overlap correction and needs the same longer window, plus a funding-charged cost model and a survivorship-frozen pool (Section 7.3's list). The research session that closed on 2026-08-12 left exactly this plan in its handoff notes: fetch 200 days, split into four sub-periods instead of two, test the funding sign out of sample, and only if the factor holds, re-cut the live exit structure from 1 ATR hour-scale exits to multi-day holds. This dossier endorses that sequence and adds two requirements to it: charge funding over the hold, and freeze pool membership at window start. If a lead survives, the committed order applies: server-side paper trading with real costs, then a written execution decision. No capital sizing is offered here: the traded expressions are below significance, quoted gross of funding and turnover, and capacity has not been modeled. Sizing talk belongs after the out-of-sample result, and this path's expected value is genuinely uncertain: that is what unproven means.

Path B: Change the premise

The research already names the alternatives if wallet-following dies entirely: mechanical flows (funding and basis capture, liquidation maps), or market making. These compete on execution quality rather than information, which means competing with professionals on infrastructure. Larger build, better-understood economics, no dependence on the cohort premise. The funding feature's wrong-signed IC (Section 7.3) is a small breadcrumb in this direction.

Path C: Sell the intelligence instead of trading it

The repository contains a costed product model from April: a $49/month subscription tier with unit economics that work at midpoint assumptions (customer lifetime value at 2.6x acquisition cost) and fail at downside ones (0.89x), requiring roughly $150K of working capital to ramp seriously and, by the project's own launch preconditions, 90+ days of published live performance data that does not yet exist. This path is real but is a different business (marketing and retention, not research) and is explicitly gated behind proving the signals are worth paying for. A signal-less variant, selling the wallet-intelligence data itself, was listed in the kill-criteria fallbacks and remains unexplored.

Path D: Stop

Listed because the project's own kill criteria list it, and because the criteria call stopping on evidence a successful outcome. The infrastructure now runs at $0/month with alarms, so "pause and let the gates accumulate data" is a legitimate low-cost variant of this path: the weekly review is 10 minutes, and the verdict clocks in Section 12.2 run themselves.

This review's recommendation

Run Path A to its verdict before considering anything else, because it is cheap, fast, and already half-done: confirm or kill the positioning factor out of sample, extend the long-hold test to 200 days with funding charged and the pool frozen, and let the recipe gates finish their counts. Stop tuning entry-copy recipes now; the measurement does not support them, and every hour spent there is taken from the leads that might pay. Hold Path C until there is something proven to sell. The decision the owner cannot delegate: if both leads die, choose between Path B's bigger build and Path D's clean stop, using the criteria already written.

ELI5

Four doors. Door A: finish testing the two promising ideas; if one works, practice with fake money, then real money, small. Door B: abandon the "follow smart traders" idea and build a different, more industrial trading machine. Door C: stop trying to trade and sell the research as a subscription, which needs real marketing money and proof the research is good. Door D: stop, having learned a lot for nearly free. The report says: finish door A first, it is nearly open; stop polishing the parts already proven not to work; and keep the promise to let the written rules, not hope, pick the door after that.

A. Glossary for external readers
  • Basis point (bps): one hundredth of a percent. 100 bps = 1%. A 50 bps edge on $10,000 is $50.
  • Rank IC (information coefficient): correlation between predicted ranking and actual outcome ranking. 0 means the prediction is useless; 1 would be perfect foresight. In quantitative finance, sustained values of 0.05 to 0.10 are considered usable.
  • t-statistic: how many standard errors a result sits from zero. Above roughly 2, chance becomes an unlikely explanation; below 2, the result is unproven. Many simultaneous tests weaken any single t-value, which is why this project split-halves everything.
  • Beta / benchmark: the portion of a return explained by the market moving. A long position in a rising market profits with zero skill; subtracting the beta-scaled market return isolates skill (alpha).
  • Coin-day clustering: fifty wallets buying one coin on one afternoon is one market observation, not fifty. Counting it fifty times manufactures false confidence.
  • Split-half test: divide the data period in two; a real effect appears in both halves, a regime artifact flips or vanishes.
  • ATR (average true range): a volatility yardstick used to size stops and targets proportionally to how much a coin normally moves.
  • Lookahead / point-in-time: a valid prediction test may only use information available before the moment of prediction. Scores rebuilt "point-in-time" honor this; the project disabled its own convenient-but-leaky variant.
  • Paper trading: simulated trading with no real money, used to validate a strategy under live conditions.
  • KV cache / cron / CI: respectively, a fast temporary data store; scheduled automated jobs; and the automation that runs on every code change.

C. Known limitations of this dossier
  • Prepared by the project's own AI assistant; independence is by evidence citation, not by organizational separation.
  • The research conclusions rest on one 120-day historical window in one market regime. Every "open" verdict in Section 7.3 is explicitly conditional on out-of-sample confirmation.
  • The research cost model charges taker fees only. No slippage and no funding are charged in the fill-study or positioning tables; funding on multi-day alt-perpetual holds is a real cost that could materially shrink the long-hold means. The live grading system does charge these; the research layer does not yet.
  • The research wallet pool is drawn from the discovery table as it exists today, and discovery is performance-conditioned, so replayed windows contain survivorship the next run must remove by freezing pool membership at window start.
  • The research program tested many hypotheses on the same cache; no formal multiple-comparisons correction is applied to the surviving t-statistics. The pre-registered out-of-sample run is the intended correction.
  • The post-correction split-half of the non-overlapping table has not been run; the split-half evidence cited for the long-hold lead comes from the overlapping version.
  • Live-outcome samples (29 graded) are below every decision threshold; Section 8's averages describe what exists and prove nothing either way.
  • Dollar capacity of the measured effects was not modeled at all; Section 13 deliberately offers no sizing.
  • The security review was code-level and read-only; no penetration testing was performed against the live deployment.
